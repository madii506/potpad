01 — overview

What this actually is

POT is a coin on pump.fun with one mechanic attached to it. The creator fee that every trade pays does not go to a wallet. It fills a pot. At the top of every hour the whole pot is sent, in dollars, to the X handle whose post about $POT got the most views during that hour.

The recipient does nothing. They do not connect a wallet, they do not paste a code into their bio, they do not sign a message, and there is no window after which the money goes back. X Money sends dollars to any X handle for free, and the handle already proved it was theirs by posting from it. There is nothing to claim.

That is the whole product. Everything below is the detail underneath it.

The short version

The thing that grows this coin and the thing that pays out are the same action. Post about it, win the hour.

02 — the rule

One pot. One winner. Every hour.

There is no application, no allowlist, and no discretion anywhere in this. Nobody decides who deserves the hour. The hour decides.

01

The fee fills it

100% of the creator fee on every $POT trade goes into the pot. Nothing is skimmed, held back for a treasury, or routed to a team wallet.

02

The hour judges it

Every public post naming $POT inside that hour is read. The one with the most views at settlement wins. Not engagement, not quality, not follower count — views.

03

X Money sends it

Dollars, to the handle, through X Money. Not tokens, not points, not a voucher.

04

The receipt records it

A public post naming the winner, the post, the view count and the amount. That post is the record, and the rundown row points at it.

Why views and not engagement

Likes and reposts can be bought for less than an hour's pot is worth, which would make the mechanic a market for fake engagement rather than a market for attention. Views are the one number on a post that tracks how many people actually saw it, and the one the platform is most motivated to keep honest. It is not a perfect number. It is the least gameable one available, and it is the number X itself publishes.

It also has the property that matters most here: a post can win on reach without its author having any followers at all, if the timeline decides to carry it.

03 — vocabulary

Words this site uses in a specific way

Where a word below appears anywhere on this site, it means exactly this and nothing looser.

The pot
The creator-fee balance accumulated since the last payout. Not a treasury, not a reserve, not a share of supply. It is emptied completely every hour — there is no rollover and no carry.
The hour
A clock hour in UTC, from :00:00 to :59:59. A post belongs to the hour it was published in, never the hour it was seen in.
Settlement
The four-minute sequence after an hour closes, in which views are read, a winner is ranked, dollars are sent and a receipt is posted. Described in full in Settlement.
Views
The view count X reports for a post, read once at settlement. The number recorded is the number at that moment. Later drift — up or down — never reopens a settled hour.
The receipt
The public post published after a payout, carrying the handle, the post, the view count and the amount. A payout with no receipt did not happen.
The rundown
The table of every settled hour since launch. It only ever grows. Nothing is back-filled, nothing is estimated, and no hour is ever removed from it.
A dash
Not zero. A dash means the site has no number, usually because the coin does not exist yet or a reader is unreachable. A zero would claim we looked and found nothing. Where this site does not know, it says so.

04 — economics

Where the money comes from

Trading fees, and nowhere else. No treasury, no allocation, no outside funding, no buyback. If nobody trades, the pot is empty, and the site will say the pot is empty rather than inventing a number.

On pump.fun the creator fee is set once, at launch, and is capped at 3.00%. All of whatever it is set to is the pot. That gives a formula with nothing hidden in it:

# every figure on the site derives from these two lines pot_per_day = volume_24h × (creator_fee_pct / 100) pot_per_hour = pot_per_day / 24

Worked through at a 1.00% fee, which is the figure the model on the front page defaults to:

What this does not promise

Volume is not a forecast. Every number in that table is a function of a volume figure you chose, not a claim about what volume will be. Until there is a pair, the front page shows dashes for all of it.

Dollars, not tokens

The pot accrues in SOL and pays out in dollars, so a conversion happens at settlement. The amount published in the receipt is the dollar amount that was actually sent, after that conversion — not the SOL value at the top of the hour. If the two differ, the receipt is the one that is true.

05 — eligibility

Which posts count

The list is short on purpose. Every line is a thing a machine can check, because a rule a person has to interpret is a rule that can be bent later.

ELIGIBLE — A POST MUST
  • be public and readable through X's own endpoints
  • name $POT in its text
  • have been posted inside the hour being settled
  • report a view count — without one it cannot be ranked
  • come from a handle not already paid today
NOT ELIGIBLE — A POST THAT
  • is private, protected, or deleted before settlement
  • was edited after the hour closed
  • comes from an account created inside the same hour
  • is a repost — the original wins, not the repost
  • comes from the $POT account itself

What the post has to say

Nothing, beyond naming $POT. There is no rule about tone, length, format or sentiment, and in particular there is no rule that it has to be complimentary. A post calling the whole thing a scam is eligible and will win the hour if it gets the views. That is not an oversight and it will not be patched.

There is no requirement to tag the account, use a hashtag, include a link, or hold the coin. Holding funds the pot; posting wins it; the two are separate.

06 — settlement

How an hour is actually settled

Four minutes, in a fixed order, starting the second the hour closes. Times are UTC and are the same every hour, including the ones nobody is watching.

T+0

The hour closes

Posts made after this second belong to the next hour, not this one. The boundary is the publish timestamp X reports, not the time the post was read.

T+1m

Views are read

Every eligible post is read once, through /api/tweet. The number recorded is the number at that moment and it is the number published. Later drift never changes a settled hour.

T+2m

The winner is ranked

Highest views takes it. A tie goes to whichever post was published earlier. If the top handle has already been paid today, it is skipped and the pot passes to the next post down.

T+3m

The dollars send

Through X Money, to the handle, in dollars. The recipient does nothing and is not asked for anything.

T+4m

The receipt posts

A public post carrying the handle, the post, the view count and the amount. That post is the record, and it is the row that appears in the rundown.

The ranking, as written

The tie-break and skip rules in one place, so there is no ambiguity about the order they apply in:

// posts: every public post naming $POT published inside [hourStart, hourEnd) const ranked = posts .filter(p => p.views !== null) // unranked without a view count .filter(p => !p.isRepost && !p.editedAfter(hourEnd)) .filter(p => p.handle !== 'POT') .sort((a, b) => b.views - a.views // 1. most views || a.createdAt - b.createdAt); // 2. tie -> published earlier const winner = ranked.find(p => !paidToday.has(p.handle)); // no eligible post -> the hour pays nothing and says so in the rundown

If no post qualifies, the hour pays nothing. It still gets a rundown row, marked as unpaid, rather than being skipped — an hour missing from the table would be indistinguishable from an hour quietly withheld.

The unpaid hour

An unpaid hour does not roll its pot forward. The fee that accrued during it goes into the next hour's pot by accident of timing, not by rule — the pot is simply whatever the fee has collected since the last payout.

07 — edge cases

When it goes wrong

Every one of these has a stated answer, decided before it happens rather than after somebody loses.

Ifthen
The winning post is deleted before the money sends The hour passes to the next post down. A post that is not there at settlement cannot be the record of anything.
The winning post is deleted after the money sends Nothing is reversed. The receipt stands and the rundown row keeps the dead link, marked as deleted.
X's view count is unavailable for a post That post cannot be ranked and is skipped. It is not treated as zero views.
X is unreachable at settlement time Settlement retries for up to fifteen minutes, then the hour is marked unpaid in the rundown with the reason stated. It is not settled later from stale numbers.
The winner's X Money is not set up The payment sits pending on X's side, where X handles it. The pot is still spent and the receipt is still published.
Two accounts post identical text No special handling. Whichever got more views wins; a tie goes to whoever published first.
An account wins twice in a day It cannot. The second win is skipped at ranking time and the pot passes down. See the rate limit.
Trading volume is zero for an hour The pot is zero and nothing sends. The rundown row says $0.00 rather than being omitted.

08 — the rate limit

Why one winner an hour

X Money allows one payment per person per day. That is the rail's rule, not a design choice made here — and the shape of the mechanic follows from it rather than fighting it.

A design that sprayed a hundred small payments an hour would hit that ceiling immediately and spend most of its time explaining why someone did not get paid. One winner an hour is 24 payments a day to 24 different handles, which sits comfortably inside the limit and never has to queue anything.

It also makes each win worth posting about. A pot split a hundred ways is a rounding error nobody screenshots; the whole hour to one person is a number worth showing people.

The consequence, stated plainly

If you win an hour, you are ineligible for the rest of that UTC day. You can still post, and your posts still count toward nothing — the hour will pass to the next handle down. This resets at 00:00 UTC.

Every endpoint this site runs on

The site has four readers and no database. Nothing is cached in a way that could go stale without saying so, and nothing writes. Each one is documented below with a button that calls it live, from your browser, right now — so you can see what it actually returns rather than what this page claims it returns.

One thing worth knowing

Only FxTwitter reports view counts, and it does not have one for every post. The syndication fallback never carries them at all. Either way views comes back as null and never as 0, and a post with a null view count cannot be ranked — which is exactly the eligibility rule above, and why it is written that way rather than as "fewest views loses".

10 — reader

GET/api/tweet

Reads one post. This is the endpoint settlement ranks on, so its behaviour around missing view counts is the whole mechanic's behaviour around them.

parammeaning
ida numeric post id
urla full x.com or twitter.com post URL — the id is parsed out of it. Either param works; id wins if both are sent.

Returns views, likes, reposts, replies, text, created, handle, name, avatar, url, exists and src. src is fxtwitter, syndication or none. Only fxtwitter can carry a view count — and even then not every post has one, so older posts commonly come back views: null from it. A null is never a zero, and a post without a view count cannot be ranked whichever source it came from. The example above is a 2022 post and returns exactly that, which is worth seeing.

11 — reader

GET/api/handle

Resolves an X handle to a display name, follower count and avatar. Used by the check tool on the front page and by the marquee.

parammeaning
ha handle, with or without the leading @

Returns handle, name, followers, verified, avatar, exists and src. A handle that does not exist returns 200 with exists: false — not a 404, because "we looked and it is not there" is a successful read.

12 — reader

GET/api/avatar

Resolves a handle's profile picture and proxies the bytes, so the browser never makes a request to X directly and no referrer leaks. Returns an image, not JSON. Cached at the edge for 24 hours.

parammeaning
ha handle, with or without the leading @

400 if the handle is missing, 404 if it cannot be resolved, 502 if the upstream image cannot be fetched.

13 — reader

GET/api/coin

Reads a Solana mint's pair from Dexscreener and runs the pot arithmetic against its real 24-hour volume. Pairs are filtered to chainId: 'solana', so a same-symbol token on another chain can never be picked up by mistake.

parammeaning
caa base58 Solana mint address, 32–44 characters. Anything else is a 400.
feecreator fee percent. Clamped to 0–3 to match pump.fun's cap. Defaults to 1.

Returns price, marketCap, liquidity, volume24, change24, pairUrl, potPerDay, potPerHour and hasPair. Where there is no pair, every figure is null — never 0.

The example above reads a different Solana token, because $POT has no mint address yet. It is there to prove the reader works, not to suggest a pair exists.

14 — errors

What a failure looks like

The readers do not invent data to avoid an error state, and they do not return zeros to look healthy. There are only three shapes of answer.

codemeaning
200The read succeeded. This includes "we looked and the thing does not exist" — those come back with exists: false or hasPair: false, because a successful lookup of a missing thing is not an error.
400The input was not the right shape: a missing handle, a post id that is not numeric, a contract address that is not base58. Nothing upstream was called.
502The upstream source could not be reached. The site shows a dash and says so out loud rather than filling the gap.

The status section on the front page probes all four readers from your own browser on every page load, so if one of them is down you find out on the page rather than from a silent dash.

16 — not on chain yet

What is not verifiable today

This is the part most sites bury. Right now the settlement rules above describe intended behaviour, not observed behaviour, because no hour has ever been settled. There is nothing on chain to check them against.

  • There is no mint address, so there is no fee income and no pot.
  • No payout has been made, so no receipt exists and the rundown has zero rows.
  • The settlement timings are a specification. Until an hour runs, they are untested in production.
  • The pot figures anywhere on this site are arithmetic on a volume number you chose. They are not a projection and not a promise.

What is checkable today: the readers, which you can call yourself from the buttons above; the arithmetic, which is four lines and shown in full; and this document, which is dated and will carry its own changelog when anything in it moves.

The standard being set

Once hours start settling, every one of them is a public post and a rundown row. An hour that is missing from the table is a broken promise you can see from the outside, which is the point of publishing the table at all.

17 — scams

Before there is an address

Read this one

$POT has no mint address at the time of writing. When one exists it will be on the front page of this site and in the pinned post, and it will appear in both at the same time. Anyone posting an address before that is scamming you, including if they say they are early, including if the account looks right.

  • Nobody involved will ever DM you an address, and nobody will ask you to send anything to receive a payout.
  • Winning an hour requires no action from you at all — so any message asking you to claim, connect, verify or sign is not from this.
  • There is no presale, no allowlist and no private round. There is nothing to be early to.
  • Payouts arrive as dollars in X Money. They do not arrive as a link.
